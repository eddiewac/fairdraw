        3 · Check a draw someone sent you

        If you were sent a link, just open it — there is nothing to do here. This is for checking a draw that arrived as text. It works everything out again from scratch and tells you whether it stands up.

        You type in the names. A number nobody can predict decides the winners. Anyone can check it was done properly — even people who don't trust you.

        Could I rig it?

        No. The names are locked in before the deciding number exists. Change one afterwards and it stops matching, and anyone checking will see that.

        Where does the number come from?

        A public network of computers that puts out a new number ten times a second. You choose a moment a few minutes away. Whatever number comes up then decides it. Nobody can know it early, because it hasn't happened yet.

        How does someone check?

        They open the link you sent. It works the whole thing out again and says whether the answer comes out the same. They don't have to take your word for anything, or ours.

        Is there a catch?

        The number is decided by about forty computers at once, and the draw uses the one that comes out lowest. Somebody running one of those computers could throw their answer away and try again — but it would only change the result about once in forty attempts, and each attempt costs them real money. Fine for a fete, a giveaway or a prize draw. We wouldn't use it to give away a house.

        Do I need an account, or to pay anything?

        No. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to install, nothing to pay.

        Built on Kaspa

        This site doesn't own the part that makes it trustworthy. The unpredictable number, the public record, and the fact a stranger can check a draw long afterwards — none of that is ours. It comes from Kaspa, a public network run by thousands of people around the world who were not asked to and are not paid by us.

        The data this page reads comes from a free public service maintained by volunteers. Recording a draw costs a twentieth of a cent, and we cover it. Everything expensive here was already there.

        If you have ever wondered what this technology is actually for, beyond people arguing about prices, this site is one small answer to that question. Worth ten minutes of curiosity if you have them.

        Keeping it free

        Every draw costs about a twentieth of a cent to record. That is paid from a single wallet, and when it runs dry the site pauses rather than asking anyone for anything.

        If you would like to top it up, you are very welcome to. You are equally welcome not to — this is not a business and there is nothing behind a paywall.

        Why you can check where it goes: the address below is the one the site spends from, and every payment into and out of it is public and permanent. If a single coin ever left it for anywhere other than recording a draw, that would be visible to anyone, forever. You do not have to take our word for it — look.
